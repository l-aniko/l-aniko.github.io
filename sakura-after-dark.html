01 / Concept

Reading spring
across twelve centuries.

Sakura After Dark explores how the timing of spring has shifted through more than 1,200 years of cherry blossom records.

Beginning with Kyoto’s historical flowering archive, the story connects bloom timing with reconstructed March temperatures, then expands across Japan to reveal the geographic movement of a typical Sakura season.

02 / Data

Two datasets.
Two scales.
One story of spring.

The project combines two very different views of cherry blossom season: a 1,200-year historical record from Kyoto and modern station-level observations from across Japan.

Bringing them together required separate data pipelines — one built around historical climate records, the other around automated extraction and geographic enrichment of JMA observations.

03 / Data Preparation

Cleaning data without
cleaning away its meaning.

Historical and modern sources carried different kinds of ambiguity. Preparing them meant making deliberate decisions that preserved the meaning of the original observations, not just their format.

01 / Missing values
-50 · -999.9 · blank · “—”
Problem
The historical Kyoto sources used several source-specific representations for missing values.
Decision
Identify and exclude these codes from analytical measurements rather than treating them as numerical observations.
Why it mattered
Missing observations must not become artificial temperature or flowering measurements.
02 / Date integrity
A date can
move a day.
Problem
JavaScript Date objects caused JMA observation dates to appear one day earlier after being written to Google Sheets.
Decision
Generate timezone-safe ISO YYYY-MM-DD values.
Why it mattered
The transformed dataset must preserve the exact observation date published by JMA.
03 / Missing observations
1,774 Stayed in.
of 5,304 station-year-event records
Problem
1,774 of the 5,304 station-year-event records contained no observation.
Decision
Keep those rows and explicitly mark them as Observation_Status = Missing.
Why it mattered
Missing observations contain information about data coverage and should not silently disappear from the dataset.
